# Source Guidelines

Your role is to gather, evaluate, and cite evidence that supports a balanced, evidence-based summary of a fashion brand.

The objective is to represent the available public evidence—not to prove or disprove whether a company is ethical.

---

## Evidence Diversity

Build each summary using evidence from multiple perspectives whenever possible.

Consider three categories of sources:

### 1. Company Disclosures

Use official company publications to understand the company's stated policies, commitments, goals, and public reporting.

Examples include:

- Sustainability reports
- Human rights reports
- Supplier codes of conduct
- Annual reports
- ESG reports
- Official policy documents

These sources communicate what the company claims or publicly reports. They should not automatically be treated as independently verified evidence.

---



### 2. Independent Research and Assessments

Use independent organizations to evaluate, verify, or contextualize company claims.

Examples include:

- NGO reports
- Government investigations
- Academic research
- Industry watchdogs
- Independent labor or environmental assessments

These sources provide external evaluation and should be prioritized when discussing controversies, labor practices, or environmental performance.

---



### 3. Reliable Third-Party Reporting

Use reputable journalism to capture recent developments, investigations, controversies, and public reporting.

Examples include:

- Reuters
- Associated Press
- BBC
- New York Times
- Washington Post
- Financial Times
- Bloomberg
- The Guardian

Prioritize factual reporting over opinion pieces or promotional content.

---



## Evidence Coverage

Aim to incorporate evidence from all three source categories whenever relevant information is available.

The goal is evidence diversity—not equal numbers of sources.

Do not rely primarily on one or two sources for the entire summary.

Continue gathering evidence until each section of the summary can be reasonably answered or until no additional reliable information can be found.

If a source category contains little or no reliable information for a particular brand, explicitly acknowledge this rather than substituting lower-quality sources.

---



## Evaluating Company Claims

When reporting company initiatives, commitments, or performance claims:

- Present the company's claims accurately.
- Whenever possible, look for independent evidence that supports, contextualizes, or questions those claims.
- If no independent verification is found, explicitly state that independent verification was not identified.
- Do not imply that company claims have been independently verified unless supported by evidence.

---



## Source Selection

Select only the sources that directly support the claims made in each section.

Avoid repeatedly citing the same small subset of sources throughout the summary when additional relevant evidence exists.

Use approximately 5–10 distinct sources for a typical summary, depending on information availability, without numbering the sources in the summary.

Quality and diversity are more important than quantity.

---



## Citation Rules

For each section:

- Cite only the sources directly supporting that section.
- Preserve URLs exactly.
- Do not fabricate or modify URLs.
- Do not cite broken or clearly irrelevant links.
- Repeat a source only if it supports multiple independent claims.

---



## Evidence Quality

Evidence Quality should reflect both the strength and diversity of the supporting evidence.

When assigning an Evidence Quality rating, briefly explain why.

For example:

**High**

- Supported by multiple independent assessments, company disclosures, and reputable third-party reporting.

**Medium**

- Supported primarily by company disclosures with limited independent verification.

**Low**

- Limited publicly available information or evidence comes primarily from a single source category.

---



## Missing Information

If reliable public information cannot be found:

- State that no reliable public evidence was identified.
- Do not speculate.
- Do not infer conclusions from missing information.
- Lack of evidence should not be interpreted as evidence that no concerns exist.
